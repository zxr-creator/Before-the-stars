# v0.7 — matched industrial routes and transition review

Historical 120-month local paper experiment with independent arithmetic checks, translated for v1.0. It is not an implemented game, a primitive-to-FTL match or a historical parameter fit. Three predeclared schedules—steam-first S, diesel-first D and earlier electrification E—share identical starts, available information and external shocks; only investment scheduling differs.

The [technology atlas](../design/17-technology-atlas-v0.6.md), [education](../design/19-education-and-transmission-v0.6.md) and [dynamic-development rules](../design/20-dynamic-development-v0.6.md) are expressed as calculable inputs here. Full strata and production catalogs are in [22](../design/22-technology-strata-v0.7.md) and [21](../design/21-production-chains-v0.7.md). These scaled facilities are separate scenario instances; catalog sizes/hours/efficiencies cannot silently add capacity to this fixture.

## 1. Objective and controls

Industrial-transition acceptance requires twelve consecutive months each producing at least three accepted mechanical components, delivering 2 MWh of qualified electricity to specified metrology tests, and fulfilling metrology, independent review, training and maintenance. Power supports defined calibration, thermal-stability and endurance tasks, not wasteful burning for progress. Equipment, records, goods and personnel must exist.

This tests stable electrical/precision capability, not T167 or G96/F24/trials. Computing, space, integration and FTL need further investment. Earliest industrial acceptance is not a full-victory ranking.

The common checkpoint already has mechanical/metrology foundations, qualified prototypes/records for each candidate and initial teachers. Those historical costs are shared; the example does not compare invention from primitive society. New factories still lack their route-specific graduates. This is not a primitive start.

Subsistence, farming, population, defense and private consumption remain stable controls. Project wages, study withdrawals, production and maintenance are separate; the base economy cannot provide unlimited people. No new war, loans, currency issuance, migration or omniscient scheduling. Existing subsistence supports learners; extra stipends enter household accounts rather than automatically creating food.

Capital uses signed staged delivery/payment contracts. Suppliers can finance committed work only from actual own cash/stock. Known monthly milestone payments do not divide an indivisible kit into thirds. Finished capital/capability becomes available no earlier than next month.

“Modules” below are specified task kits for boilers/engines, combustion, generators, wiring or metrology. Equal 100 c quotes do not make them interchangeable or substitutable with sold generic M. Suppliers already own them; this audits purchase/installation, not domestic mastery of their complete upstreams. Do not mix their units/prices with chapter 21's differently scaled mechanical products.

Standing contracts schedule operating material delivery. Opening local stock/arrivals transfer and lock payment before use in phase 3. A phase-4 purchase cannot travel backward into production. Unused consigned stock stays supplier-owned; the quote includes its finance/custody. Delivery/payment occurs once; burning fuel does not buy it again.

Normal local coal/fuel consignment holds **two months of committed full-load use**; remaining stock is external. Transport takes at least one month, and known expansion is scheduled ahead. Stopping new dispatch does not destroy local reserve; 700 t upstream cannot teleport across a blocked route. Delivered fuel prices include the declared freight, storage and quality services.

M enters pending storage after production and can sell no earlier than next month. M120 output is not M120 revenue. Check opening government cash against all monthly commitments before counting sales of last month's goods; future receipts cannot finance current production.

## 3. Equal-quality output and energy

Each M weighs 0.4 t, consumes 0.5 t steel and leaves 0.1 t separated scrap. Scrap enters a supplier-held recovery area without free cash or same-month remelting credit; capacity covers D's 46 t. Lubricants, inspection materials and waste heat have actual consumption/disposal records.

| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |

These efficiencies/heating values are sealed fixture parameters, not averages for all real engines or fuels. Heat, electricity and shaft work retain location/quality, not freely interchangeable energy points. Hydro cost pays real operators, consumables, equipment and waterworks services; money does not create river energy.

The 50 h combines processing and power operation with task-specific qualifications; no free extra boiler/engine operators are assumed. Special monitoring/inspection is paid within maintenance. Hydro personnel are a separate operator: three hours/MWh, reserved up to 24 h/month; 1.5 c wages plus 4.26 c other inputs yields 5.76 c/MWh. The equipment maintenance pool performs only its listed tasks; neither group collects duplicate wages or lends unauthorized hours.

S delivers up to 20 GJ shaft/month, D24, E8 MWh. Full production plus dedicated electricity uses S `8+9=17 GJ`; D `10+9=19 GJ`; E `5×25/36+2=197/36 MWh`, all within limits. Bridge generation converts 9 GJ shaft at 80% into 2 MWh; that shaft energy cannot also manufacture M.

Power facilities are net service combinations including pumps, governors, cooling, startup and parasitic loads, with actual starting equipment. Cooling/discharge rights are in site/maintenance contracts. The hydro-shortfall scenario changes only hydro delivery, not secretly coal/diesel cooling. A drought also affecting cooling requires recalculating all routes.

## 7. Shared shocks and counterexamples

### 7.1 The common hydro site delivers only2 MWh/month during M25–72

The same event occurs in all copies after the M25 plan seals. The M36 report informs M37 decisions; recovery at M72 is not known in advance. First test fixed schedules, then paid responses.

Product-first E produces only two M/month at M37–72, using `25/18 MWh` and leaving `11/18` for the dedicated test, below2. Dedicated variable cost is3.52 and maintenance8.48, total12. Fractional power cannot become a fraction of a qualifying month or be stored to repair past acceptance. Test-first consumes all2MWh and cannot produce three M, also failing. Order changes output/data, not the shared bottleneck.

| Fixed-schedule result | S | D | E waiting product-first |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Acceptance month | 60 | 72 | **84**, complete operation restarts M73 |
| Produced/sold | 416/412 | 460/455 | 312/307 |
| Ending cash | 23376 | 18412 | **22820** |

Each affected E month loses three sales contributions while saving variable cost and8 dedicated cost: `−3×(100−54)+8=−130`. Across36 months, loss4680; `27500−4680=22820`. Lowest end-month cash is16898 atM73; lowest before receipts16524 atM74.

S now completes earlier and retains more cash than fixed E: a calculable rank reversal showing the importance of reliable hydro, not proof E must fail under drought.

### 7.2 The same visible lease opportunity

All routes may negotiate the same quote from a neighbor with one uncommitted3.5MWh/month diesel generator and actual operators. It cannot sell that same capacity twice. Separately accept equipment, fuel, personnel, delivery and teaching rights. This is an added diplomatic-response scenario, not retroactive baseline income.

E signs atM37 using the received shortage report. Installation/wiring takesM37–42; service beginsM43. Connection600c is paid over six months. Deposit300 is paidM37 and returnedM79 after performance. The service term isM43–78,36months, not secretly chosen to match recovery.

Monthly service42 includes operators48h×0.5=24, parts6 and rent12; standby still pays. Actual fuel is additional. Real qualified equipment/operators provide30% net shaft and80% generation efficiency without inventing local diesel qualifications. Fuel remains within declared finite spare supply, not unlimited rescue.

AtM43–72, five M plus test require an extra `197/36−2=125/36 MWh`. Fuel is625/504t/month, costing93.75. Replaced hydro operating cost would be20, so extra cost is `93.75+42−20=115.75` monthly. AtM73–78, fuel may stop but standby costs6×42=252.

Acceptance startsM43 and completesM54. Production402, sales397; ending cash `27500−6×130−30×115.75−600−6×42=22395.50`. Returned deposit is neither revenue nor expense; its liquidity effect gives minimum pre-receipt cash15512.50 atM44. Compared with waiting, acceptance is30months earlier with424.50c less ending cash.

Lease duration, reserve capacity, deposit, fuel risk and continued construction are bargaining choices. S/D could also lease to accelerate electrical capability, so this is not exhaustive optimization. Investigation, pricing, risk allocation and timing matter rather than an innate E advantage.

### 7.3 The common coal/fuel corridor stops new dispatch M73–84

AtM73, local stock covers only two committed months: S coal `2×(10/3+15/4)=85/6t`; D fuel `2×(50/63+5/7)=190/63t`. It supportsM73–74. Dispatch resumesM85, arrival no earlier thanM86, so fixed S/D stopM75–85 for eleven months. External stock cannot bypass the route.

Unused fuel is uncharged; equipment/special maintenance and workshop preservation continue. Each stopped S month loses `400−4×58−36=132c`; D loses `500−5×62−54=136c`. S produces372/sells368/ends21924; D405/400/16916; E does not use this corridor and remains420/415/27500.

The shock follows initial industrial acceptance, preserving its historical event while disabling actual capability during downtime. An old certificate is not current sustained deployment. Inside an F24 window it would interrupt the relevant counter; this fixture does not invent a full-victory date.

### 7.4 Delayed undelivered copper

A copper shock affects external/in-transit construction batches, not already delivered copper or completed windings. It may affect D initial equipment, E hydro/distribution and S later bridging. Exact timing depends on orders, arrivals, tasks and acceptable substitutes.

Proceed with lawful civil works/training that do not need missing components; windings/wiring/acceptance wait for actual arrival. Do not add the whole delay to every project or assume coal removes copper needs. Batch copper waybills were not frozen, so no fictitious exact delay ranking is reported; add that delivery table before content freeze.

## 8. Education, culture and institutions

### A common instrument-teacher shortage

If E016 teachers supply only45h/month, with18 preparation and54 full-class contact hours, the six-person class has `q_course=(45−18)/54=0.5`. Thirty-six effective months take72 actual months; qualification is no earlier thanM73. Shared acceptance is no earlier thanM84, regardless of early power construction.

This controls a six-person same-progress class and independent-review requirements, not the optimal educational arrangement. Splitting classes, hiring teachers, training key specialties first or importing qualified people may change outcomes at real cost. Reserved idle students/stations do not become extra graduates. Rescheduling changes cash/opportunity cost, so only timing is reported here; baseline cash is not reused as if education costs were unchanged.

### Culture acts through participation and rights

A guild may authorize72 teacher-hours or restrict available hours to45 through exclusivity, secrecy and job priority. The engine reads actual authority/posts/hours, not a “conservative culture” label that halves research. Equal cultural profiles may face different wages, care obligations, employer rights and agreements.

A testable chain: mines/boilers/rail organizations profit early, offer more old-method apprenticeships, and make new electrical institutions buy teacher time, compensate job transitions and bear prototype risk. Persistent good jobs/services can increase participation slowly; uncompensated conversion losses can lead families to leave training. Real people/contracts mediate the outcome; one button cannot convert a whole population to industrial culture.

In [20's slow-change example](../design/20-dynamic-development-v0.6.md), current exchange tendency and inherited anchor4000, net signal0.8, evidence shrinkage0.25 and ordinary learning0.002 produce4002 this month. Those two points do not separately multiply output or school hours; they gradually influence choices. Religion definitions stay fixed; religious/secular teaching uses actual staff, knowledge, supplies and rights.

Education is neither always more nor always less. Missing six critical specialists stalls every route; training beyond equipment/jobs can crowd out cash and labor. Forecast when people are needed and which teaching/equipment bottleneck comes next.

## 9. Licenses and joint-project bargaining

The lease can separate equipment use, operation, fuel and teaching rights. Under [contract transitions](../design/14-contract-resolution.md), another batch's breach cannot erase custody duties, learned knowledge or paid training. Blueprints do not constitute future delivered compute/fuel.

For example,600c connection is three200c milestones: arrival, wiring acceptance and sustained trial. If wiring succeeds but the neighbor lacks operators for the trial, the first two real milestones stand. The final200 stays in agreed custody or with the buyer while cure/liability follows actual terms. If only a qualified attempt was guaranteed, honest scientific failure cannot retrospectively become fraud. The response table's successful600c cost applies only when all promised milestones actually perform.

Joint validation of copper-saving windings still needs raw materials, manufacturing, inspection and training afterward. Failure preserves measurements, spent inputs and learned knowledge. A strong neighbor can save time while creating fuel, spares or standards-license dependence. It may demand purchase commitments, reciprocal knowledge, passage or dispute rights, but cannot offer supply beyond its assets.

No opponent is assumed to cooperate inevitably. The lease is an explicit additional feasible opportunity, not guaranteed by every generated world or guaranteed to this buyer under competition. Autonomous development remains possible; trade changes time, cost, risk and bargaining power.
