# 0.4 Integrated Cases and Exploit Review

This is an archived document-design and local-arithmetic review, not a game run. The [0.3 four-civilization walkthrough](02-campaign-walkthrough.md) remains historical; its two trials no longer suffice for 0.4 military-wide FTL acceptance. These cases connect the new rules while distinguishing assumptions from calculations. Version 1.0 supersedes fine fertility objects with [32](../design/32-population-and-education-v1.0.md) and permits only competitive, single-seat victory. Cooperation in the examples remains a strategy, not shared scoring eligibility.

## Case A: Urban expansion, education, and mobilization cannot all grow without limit

Riverbend already has agriculture and a city; Stormforge needs food and instruments; Tideport supplies transport and training; Highland controls an alternative mineral route. Their technology and population arise from actual earlier construction. The following extracts local months without skipping primitive-to-settled development for free.

Riverbend has 10,000 people, 300 births and 280 deaths in one year, and no net migration: growth is only 20. It does not have a permanent 3% growth rate. In a separate crisis branch, 180 births, 100 direct war deaths, 500 other deaths, 700 emigrants, and 20 immigrants leave an actual 8,900. At 75% registration coverage, visible records show about 6,675. The agent should investigate lost contact, migration, and coverage rather than call the entire 3,325 registration difference war casualties.

Excessive extraction, withdrawal of irrigation maintenance labor, and interrupted imports could jointly produce that crisis. These are hypotheses for full simulation, not conclusions established by the table. Lower taxes, food transport, a ceasefire, and restored maintenance may help while sacrificing current military spending and engineering speed. No dynasty automatically collapses when it reaches a specified age.

Stormforge has 1000 full monthly labor equivalents: agriculture 600, mining 200, manufacturing 150, care 50. Enrolling 60 students and drawing 10 teachers from manufacturing leaves agriculture 540 and manufacturing 140, with other uses unchanged and the total still 1000. At 2u of food per agricultural equivalent, food output falls from 1200 to 1080u against demand of 1100u, a monthly deficit of 20u. Tideport could exchange food/teachers for future market access, while Stormforge could instead use reserves to stagger training.

Two countries with 10,000 registered residents do not have equal military strength. In the [war example](../design/13-war-resolution.md), actual willingness, instructors, camps, and equipment limit one side to 400 new trainees and the other to 700. Neither immediately obtains advanced troops that month. Renaming, repeated recruitment, or simultaneous full-time study and military service cannot bypass this.

## Case B: Licensing, failure, and alternative suppliers

Stormforge buys a 180c process transfer from Tideport: rights 20, documentation 60, and result-guaranteed pilot production 100. The first two items pass acceptance and pay 80. The pilot fails despite the agreed correct inputs, and cure fails, so the remaining 100 returns. Stormforge retains learned documentation without gaining production capability. Tideport cannot collect everything merely by stating the technology's name, and is not automatically liable for out-of-contract applications.

If the pilot was predefined as independently useful installation worth 40 and stable output worth 60, accepted installation raises cumulative payment to 120 and leaves only 60 refundable. An indivisible project cannot claim “40% completion” to obtain the same payout. Stormforge may instead buy instruments first, run a small trial purchase, research independently, or seek Highland as an alternative. The better choice depends on money, time, input compatibility, and counterparty credibility.

In a branch where the buyer deliberately destroys the acceptance bench, a presigned and separately escrowed reservation fee of 10 and performance security of 20 produces total funding 210 = accepted rights/documentation 80 + incurred inspection 10 + evidenced security execution 20 + returned unaccepted engineering payment 100. Without prior terms and evidence, an omniscient referee cannot secretly punish the buyer. Staging, witnesses, and real escrow manage contractual risk.

Three joint-research members contribute 180 in shares of 90/60/30. Actual spending is 165. A negative result does not refund consumed services; the remaining 15 returns as 7.5/5/2.5. Members retain failure data according to actual access and vested rights. Later nonpayment may stop new services and deliveries, not erase learned knowledge. Messages, acceptance, and payment use unique settlement credentials; retransmission cannot collect twice.

## Case C: Military advantage and occupation costs

With identical roles, training, command, morale, and terrain, 1200 troops one technology layer higher face 1600 lower-layer troops. Their capacities are 3000 versus 1600. The 0.4 simultaneous formula gives unrounded first-month incapacitation of approximately 33.3913 versus 83.4783. Technological superiority has a real effect rather than being narrative randomness.

If the advanced side's supply falls to 0.45, its capacity becomes 1350. Unrounded incapacitation is approximately 52.0678 versus 58.5763, while inadequate supply also damages later morale. A few advanced troops cannot upgrade every lower-tech formation. Unlimited splitting cannot create attacks or evade residual casualties. Deaths, wounded people, and prisoners have distinct accounts.

Attack capacity 200, defense 50, and node resistance 100 imply progress 30, limited by the monthly ceiling of 20. Capturing the node requires at least 5 months, followed by actual takeover and at least 6 months of stable control. The conqueror owes resident services, resettlement, and administration; expulsion cannot shrink victory responsibilities. Insufficient qualified administrators or supplies can make negotiated autonomy attractive. Conquest is not guaranteed to be faster than cooperation.

## Case D: Population stagnation without war or famine

A skilled urban Tideport has 100,000 people, 700 annual births, 900 deaths, and net immigration of 50, ending at 99,850. No cultural-penalty event is required; age structure and household behavior may jointly lower births. Long trajectories must still validate the mechanism. This identity alone does not establish causation.

The historical [population specification](../design/12-population-and-development.md) illustrated a group's monthly new pregnancies rising from 4.08 to 6.07 after service improvements. That fine pregnancy-state implementation is archived; 1.0 uses the explicit aggregate delayed-birth example in [32](../design/32-population-and-education-v1.0.md). In either model, extra births cannot arrive before the nine-month lag, and must then mature and learn. Birth policy cannot solve this month's arms-industry vacancies. Job reassignment, adult training, immigration, automation, or cooperation may help sooner, each with a cost. Family aspirations, care capacity, and education opportunities are not one “good/bad culture” score.

## Case E: Total conquest, military-wide FTL, and reward

Independent strategy branches with illustrative fixed horizon H=1200:

| Branch | Main sequence | Complete endpoint and return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early conquest | Conquest at M600; engineering, officers, manufacture, and maintenance meet every window only at M900 | If all conditions hold, T=900 and R=1.25; M600 is not terminal |
| Cooperative construction first | Sustained FTL at M800; actual integrated governance completes its 96-month window only at M950 | If capability and trials still qualify, T=950 and R≈1.20833; only the actual controlling seat can win under 1.0 |
| Prototype finish-line rush | Only two trials completed at M700; manufacturing, replacement, and military-wide deployment are missing | No victory and no FTL-distance reward |
| Failure by horizon | Requirements unmet by H, or earlier self-destruction/elimination | Total return -1; early termination does not waive time cost |

The first two are time-evaluation examples, not proof that war is easier or better. Early conquest may destroy skills and supplies, delaying FTL. If federation members refuse to transfer highest authority, cooperation may remain unresolved. A complete match must generate these conditions endogenously.

## Exploit regression checklist

| Attack | Required property |
| --- | --- |
| Create a hundred recruitment/research projects | Actual candidates and experimental trials cannot duplicate; resources, willingness, and remainders reconcile by actual process |
| Count wounded people as dead, then replace them | Wounded, dead, captured, and returning people are uniquely recorded |
| Occupy a battlefield under multiple flags | One actual controller shares frontage and output; flag count does not add attacks |
| Use cultural/religious labels as army-wide bonuses | Participation, training, and organization must change first; reports hide response weights |
| Spend reserved money after a local offer expires | Nothing unfreezes without valid release credentials; distant activation cannot double-spend |
| Demand full refunds after research failure | Distinguish actual services, delivery guarantees, and result guarantees; unused assets differ from sunk costs |
| Learn documentation, then default | Learned knowledge remains; real credit risk persists; staged disclosure and escrow control future delivery |
| Shrink the army, borrow ships, or temporarily buy two engines | Fixed tasks and actual responsibilities remain; 24 months of capability, manufacture, and replacement are still required |
| Kill people, lose contact, or redraw districts to shrink denominators | Actual population differs from registration; disorder and resettlement obligations survive deleted records |
| Earn reward through extra windows, empty actions, or training truncation | Reward follows simulation months; all failures total -1; `truncated` is not an invented environment terminal |
| Obtain true causes of low fertility or refusal to serve for free | Reports contain surveys, accounts, and sourced inferences; unknowns remain unknown |

## Closed specifications and remaining validation

Core war and contract transitions, default deadlines, simultaneity, resources, and responsibilities have reviewable baselines. Population, military-wide acceptance, reward, and panels are also specified. Remaining content is not left to arbitrary implementation: equipment and FTL recipes, map capacities, stage-specific task inputs, and population parameters must be enumerated during content freeze.

This review checks local accounts, logic, and document consistency. Running software must still validate long-run population stability, transferability of historical phenomena, adversarial strategies, information noninterference, multicore consistency, and performance targets. Narrative walkthroughs cannot substitute for any missing evidence.

Historical checks reported at the time of 0.4: 32 Markdown files, 128 local links, and 18 unique action classes; fences and trailing whitespace passed. Twenty paper-arithmetic groups covered population/pregnancy, nutrition recovery, urban–rural hours, licensing/security/research refunds, custody recovery, barter, battle losses, technology differences, time reward, and shaping cancellation. The temporary checking script was not added to the project; JSON Schema and game implementation were unchanged. Final review corrected repeated nutrition-recovery deductions, fictional population for unmanned equipment, morale reset through demobilization/recruitment, and contract recovery from `blocked`. These historical counts are not current 1.0 validation results.
