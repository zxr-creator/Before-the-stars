# A four-civilization paper walkthrough

Historical v0.3 scenario. v0.4 subsequently required military FTL manufacturing, deployment and continuous capability; two trials alone no longer suffice. See [v0.4 cases](03-v0.4-cases.md) and the current [v1.0 plan](../design/30-complete-plan-v1.0.md). Historical cooperative alternatives below are not selectable current modes.

This is a designer-visible mechanism test, not an initial agent briefing or program log. World conditions, choices, culture changes and late outcomes are conditional author-created examples. Only explicit local accounts/time checks were arithmetically verified; no complete equilibrium or battle win rate was calculated.

## Scenario

Four communities occupy camps/contact areas across six regions each, on a 24-region map, without modern comprehensive tax authority. There are 80 twelve-month decisions: R1=M1–12, R80=M949–960. Content is heavily compressed and does not claim primitive-to-FTL development takes 80 real years. Do not infer population/cultural speeds from this artificial story. Terminal rules are sealed at opening and initially unknown to the seats.

| Civilization | Initial population | Known advantage | Shortage/motive |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Riverbend A | 12,000 | Valley, water, gatherable food | Seasonal supply; stabilize food and shelter |
| Mistforge B | 10,000 | Exposed stone, forests, toolmaking experience | Fewer food opportunities; seek exchange |
| Tideport C | 8,000 | Harbor and coastal food | Small hinterland; explore coastal contacts |
| Highland D | 10,000 | Upland herding/gathering and passes | Dispersed settlements; investigate access and resources |

All begin without religion or an abstract scientific-development agenda. They know local contacts, goods and practical problems. Each has a more costly autonomous technological route; another player's refusal cannot permanently prevent development. Population totals span camps, not prebuilt cities. Mines, guilds, docks, treasuries and industrial goals must emerge rather than appear in initial prompts.

`c` is an example accounting unit equal to 1000 minimum currency units. `u` is a batch of the specific product defined in its table and is not added across products. Treasury is not all social property.

## R1–R8: subsistence to settlement surplus

Use actual communal reserves and voluntarily organized labor to build storage, repair tools and investigate food/routes. Zero cash is possible; participants still eat. Assume A adopts early farming after R2–R4 planting/storage trials and then builds granaries/channels; B exchanges actual timber/tools for food; C maintains simple landing facilities; D improves passes. No population-objective reward creates grain. This is compressed test content, not a claim about agricultural origins.

A settlement begins recurring mutual-aid meetings using hours and stored food, gradually forming a local belief movement. Identical terrain elsewhere without participation does not simultaneously generate religion. A pantheon appears only at R5 and a maintained sacred site at R8, without automatic formal founding.

B spends equivalent labor/materials on apprentices and housing; D remains secular. If later basic teaching were available only through A's sacred site, the example would expose religion as mandatory. Nonreligious organizations must also provide records, teaching and relief.

Exploit check: splitting one movement into three organizations cannot claim three founding positions. Projects share the same training record. Cancellation does not refund eaten food; only actually unspent reservations can release.

## R9–R18: cities are more than agricultural bonuses

Assume surplus and institutions have accumulated and trading areas have adopted explicit money with opening issuance accounts. Primitive communities are not replaced by free modern governments.

C wants artisans. A district with 700 people has housing for 1000 but reliable water/sanitation for 800. Attracting 300 more does not create 300 fully productive artisans: at least 200 face service gaps, with travel, health and housing affecting migration/work. C invests in services and training first. B expands industry and encounters water, housing and waste conflicts. These need different urban/industrial projects, not simply more farms.

Agents see applications, wages, occupancy, water and downtime records. They do not receive cultural causal tables or private values. They may test whether housing helps recruitment using actual surveys.

**Production conservation:** raw material 12u, input 2u per product, machine capacity 20 gives at most six products. Multiplying that by efficiency two would produce twelve using an implied 24u and is forbidden.

**Shared rural/education planning:** B has 1000 full-time-equivalent monthly labor shares: agriculture 600, mining 200, manufacturing 150, care 50. Taking 60 agricultural students and ten qualified manufacturing teachers leaves agriculture 540, mining 200, manufacturing 140, care 50, study 60, teaching 10: still 1000, with lost production.

With other inputs sufficient and two food units per agricultural share, next-month output falls from 1200 to 1080u against 1100u demand. Twenty units must come from old stock or arriving goods; 100u reserves become 80u. Training is not automatically self-financing. Distant mines need food transport, and teaching materials/facility maintenance add costs. B can stagger intake, import, improve verified farming or delay mines, not instantly create skilled graduates.

This course needs 36 effective training months and final task validation. Supply, attendance and practice determine completion. C may exchange materials for graduates' voluntary employment; B may offer retention terms. Neither purchases ownership of people.

## R19–R26: contracts, communication and research

C has reproducible experimental practice while B emphasizes experience. C's scientific-method capability reveals an abstract research agenda; B can investigate faults without automatically receiving C's goals or methods.

For instruments, B can buy products, license and validate a process, experiment itself or delay. C knows one method poorly matches B's materials; B sees successful samples. The contract cannot reveal suitability automatically or pay the entire engineering price for a rights certificate.

The 180c transfer separates legal rights 20c, tested records delivery 60c and prototype validation under specified inputs 100c. Failed validation does not erase B's learning; the unmet final milestone is unpaid. Spent materials/training are not refunded. Without credible custody/acceptance, B can trial a small purchase or develop independently.

At R20's end/M240, both sign the same received offer. Each signature takes two months to the registry, arriving M242; formation is no earlier than M242, not M241. An expiry at M241 prevents retroactive signing and releases reservations only under applicable release rules; communication costs remain spent. Withdrawal arriving with the last signature has the public priority specified by the contract rules.

## One industrial month's public cash account

A separate snapshot has no new-signature delay and opens with 600c and no omitted reservations. Food is already deliverable from local old stock, not foreign goods treated as spot supply.

| Step | Available | Contract custody | Temporary monthly reserve | Paid | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Opening | 600 | 0 | 0 | 0 | Government cash only |
| Reserve contract 180, operations 100, food cap 100 | 220 | 180 | 200 | 0 | No double spending |
| Pay wages/research/maintenance 100 | 220 | 180 | 100 | 100 | Recipients gain 100 |
| Buy 4u at 20, below limit 25 | 240 | 180 | 0 | 180 | Pay 80, release unused 20 |
| Receive actual month-end taxes 90 | 330 | 180 | 0 | 180 | Taxpayers lose 90; no creation |

Government holdings are `330+180=510c=600−100−80+90`. Counterpart accounts move correspondingly. The 180 remains held pending acceptance and cannot finance another project.

The received 90 can fund next month. Twelve months of future taxes cannot be opening cash, and beginning work need not require twelve monthly budgets upfront. Standing purchases regenerate with actual funding; an unfunded project pauses without hidden overdraft.

Splitting a 4u order into 400 orders gives no extra remainder rights. Self-trading food does not improve resident supply or farm agenda support. Genuine stockpiling remains legal and costs cash/opportunities.

## R27–R38: specialization, disruption and alliances

B's energy needs make A's food and C's port important. D's minerals acquire value through a realized process demand, not an initial “rare” label, and D seeks training for supply.

B needs ten comparable energy-service batches monthly. Existing supply rises to 22c each. D offers 18c, with 40c setup and one month's delay. Electrical conversion costs 600c, takes two months and then costs 10c/batch. All buy at 22 while waiting to preserve comparable service.

| Horizon | Existing supply | D alternative | Electrical conversion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 months | 660 | 620 | 1140 |
| 12 months | 2640 | 2240 | 2040 |

Formulas: `220H`; `220+40+180(H−1)`; `440+600+100(H−2)`. This excludes terminal capital value, common costs and finance, so is not a full policy-value function. Residual value, prices and diverted builders can change the comparison.

Expecting a short dispute, B selects D and three months' reserves while delaying conversion. Expecting a long blockade could favor conversion. C reduces its quote after seeing a substitute. A trades reliable grain delivery for training without indefinitely financing escorts. Neither permanent trade nor permanent self-development is universally prescribed.

## R39–R48: limited war and cultural change

B attacks D's pass. In the stipulated contact snapshot, B has 1200 actually trained/equipped people one technology layer higher; D has 1600 at the lower layer, with otherwise equal readiness/roles. Full supply gives relative base strength 3000 versus 1600. B at supply factor 0.45 has 1350 versus 1600. Numbers/supply can offset technology without a lucky die. These values do not determine losses or conquest without the later campaign model.

Actual personnel are not national population. Industry shortages, some followers' refusal of this offensive, distrust and training gaps may limit B; D's defensive participation may differ. Soldiers leave jobs and need support rather than continuing full-time mining or study.

C may escort B, raise fees or stay neutral. A demands peace rather than prolonged grain losses. B's artisans/residents face downtime and prices. Peace takes effect at R46, canceling old offensive orders; a stale order cannot provide an innocent excuse for renewed attack.

Assume B later has 12,000 registered people after occupational migration/divergence. This is a conservation snapshot, not a cultural-model prediction:

| Cultural profile | Registered | Known religion | No formal religion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Valley tradition | 5000 | 1500 | 3500 |
| Furnace tradition | 4000 | 1000 | 3000 |
| Civic tradition | 3000 | 1500 | 1500 |
| Total | 12000 | 4000 | 8000 |

Culture shares are 4167/3333/2500 basis points; religious shares about 3333/6667. These are separate distributions of the same people, not 24,000 people. Profile names are not one-to-one occupations, and urban residents do not automatically share one culture.

Losses may strengthen restrictions on passage or demands for reliable escorts. Agents see opinion, participation, prices and attributed events, not “war reduced openness by X.”

Suppose a privileged organization has long avoided maintenance obligations and facilities have deteriorated. This is a local harmful practice, not a permanent property of every resident. The failed campaign reduces its credibility and creates support for repair/training reform. Fast opinion shifts do not instantly change durable tendencies from 6000 to 2000. Repeated small defeats cannot farm awakening bonuses. Sustained reform depends on subsequent resources, teaching and services.

## R49–R64: AI, joint research and actual control

A has energy/cash, B manufacturing, C data/network and D selected materials/sites. Joint research does not automatically create the final political union.

Rated monthly compute is 100, but power supports 60, cooling 50 and talent/maintenance 70: actual capacity is 50. Training reserves 30 and existing inference 15, leaving only five to promise abroad. Rated 100 and actual 50 cannot both be sold. External Codex/Claude reasoning budgets do not increase.

C tries a token first contribution for perpetual access. New private-stage results require the signed contribution conditions. Previously received knowledge remains and public spillovers can be legal, but unperformed rights cannot be fabricated.

Funds enter a separate project account; A cannot count the same cash toward a new fleet. Members may exit for another route and retain sunk costs. Distribution of cooperation's gains is a real negotiation.

## R65–R80: FTL is not victory

The main branch is single-winner. A first completes theory/engineering, discovers the endgame and trials at R72. B/C/D may sell materials or join temporary defense without surrendering permanent administrative, budget and security powers.

Assume later war, peace and administration make A the actual world government only at R75. The military outcome is a scenario input, not a derived inevitability. Eliminated seats need not sign, but residents and resettlement responsibilities persist.

M889–960 gives only 72 continuous governance months. Trials at R76/M912 and R78/M936 are 24 months apart; the earlier R72 trial lies outside this governance period. At R80 the historical governance test remains unmet: **unresolved**, with no improvised points champion for the strongest state.

A separately declared branch integrated at R73/M865 would have 96 months by M960 if trials and services held. This demonstrates governance timing, not that opponents would easily permit it. Do not extend the original match and retroactively award victory. Under current rules the additional F24/manufacturing/deployment tests must also be satisfied; this older example alone does not establish a v1.0 win.

The old shared-future variant could encourage earlier voluntary integration. It represented different incentives and has been removed from v1.0; it cannot justify assuming competitors will help A win.
