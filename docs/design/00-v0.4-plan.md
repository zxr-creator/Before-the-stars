# Before the Stars — v0.4 design plan

Historical baseline. The current entry point is the [v1.0 plan](30-complete-plan-v1.0.md). This English translation preserves the original design reasoning; later population simplification, competitive-only victory, four-seat scope and clock rules override superseded details. Monthly timing and state ownership were subsequently expanded in the [v0.5 engine plan](00-v0.5-engine-plan.md).

This is designer material, not a player/agent package. v0.4 changed documents only. Its goal was long-term civilization building, complex diplomacy and inspectable fast feedback in one lightweight mechanism set. Initial numerical values supported paper calculation and later calibration; they did not demonstrate historical reproduction or balance.

## Product objective

Develop from a primitive nonreligious community through changing technological/cultural opportunities to a political entity able to govern the world continuously and pass **FTL military-system acceptance**. Conquest, federation and technical cooperation are means; every path requires FTL. The selected reward uses full-victory time, with pure FTL time diagnostic only.

Evaluation concerns multistep building, opportunity cost, contract performance, talent pipelines and uncertainty. It does not reward kill count, nominal population, transient GDP or the most researched nodes. Valid unconventional strategies remain legal. An exploit bypasses inputs, responsibilities, observation or acceptance; surprising the designer is not itself an exploit.

## Seven systems linked through three stocks

1. **Material and capability stocks:** people, food, equipment, land, energy and transport services, changed by real production and loss.
2. **Knowledge and organizational stocks:** skills, reproducible records, engineering validation, instructors, schools, factories and execution networks. Some deteriorate without support; knowledge does not disappear overnight.
3. **Rights and expectations:** property, due obligations, licenses, affected-resident responsibilities, cultural habits and attributed trust. Renaming or changing flags does not erase them.

All compete for finite hours, goods, cash and locations through simultaneous monthly proposals and stable settlement. Complementarity, delay and allocation create depth without a universally convertible civilization-point currency.

| Chain | Long-term decision | Possible consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography/resources → land/transport → rural/urban surplus | Irrigation, dispersed production, roads, imports or autonomy | Tax burdens, pollution, dependency and maintenance |
| Surplus → education/experiments → technology → capacity | Consumption versus next-generation training; buy equipment or learn to make it | Skilled shortages, obsolete capital, migration and temporary food loss |
| Urban jobs/housing/care → family decisions → age structure | Services, training, migration and family support | Long education/care lead times; population policy creates no immediate workers |
| Industrial returns → faction resources → institutions/culture → adoption | Compensation, shared gains, verification, transmission and reform pace | Monopoly, rent seeking, refusal and nominal adoption without execution |
| Culture/religion/education → mobilization/command | Mission legitimacy, training, officers and logistics | Workers leave production; participation differs by mission; organizational friction |
| Trade/licenses → knowledge/equipment → alternatives | Trial purchases, staged licenses, joint research and reserve capacity | Lock-in, breach, spillovers, failed validation and disputes |

This is referee-mechanism explanation, not automatic agent knowledge. Both people and agents see accounts, reports, samples, real obligations and known process conditions. Inferring relationships is part of play.

## Population and stagnation

Population uses neither a fixed growth rate nor a mandatory logistic curve. Births, deaths, migration and aging settle separately; desired family size and actual support differ. Population, extraction and maintenance can exhaust agricultural surplus. Shocks, war and service failure can raise deaths and displacement. Longer life with expensive child-rearing, limited time and delayed births can also produce low fertility and aging.

Culture affects families, occupations and obligations without ranking national worth. The same family norm yields different outcomes with affordable housing/care versus expensive housing and excessive work. Religion acts through services and duties, not a name-based fertility or military multiplier. See [population](12-population-and-development.md), with the simpler current representation in [32](32-population-and-education-v1.0.md).

Technological stagnation does not follow national labels. Check actual problems, communicable knowledge, qualified skills, instruments/materials, sustained investment, validation/application sites and acceptable organization. A missing necessary link cannot be solved directly by more money or people, but alternatives, conversion, licensing, trade, training and negotiation may remove it. War can create demand while destroying talent and networks; it is not a universal research accelerator.

## War and contracts become settleable

[War resolution](13-war-resolution.md) defines recruitment, training, organization, shared command, deployment, battle, losses, retreat and occupation month by month. Culture/religion affect actual participation, obligations and cooperation; education supplies real skills. No final national cultural multiplier is added. A blind three-role equilibrium may exist; missions, preparation, supply and timing change useful composition.

[Contract resolution](14-contract-resolution.md) separates proposal, delivery, formation, custody, performance, acceptance, cure, dispute, termination and closure. Scientific failure is not automatically breach. Nondelivery, nonpayment, interrupted contributions, third-party obstruction and custody failure differ in responsibility. Knowledge is not rolled back; spent inputs are not refunded; unspent assets return only under executable conditions. One sum cannot exist in multiple stages simultaneously.

## Lightweight choices

The historical scope was four civilizations/24 regions for the prototype and 4–8/48–72 for the full game. v1.0 replaces this with four total civilizations and 48 standard regions. Aggregate settlements, age/skill cohorts, regional campaigns and bounded contract/acceptance templates remain. No individual psychology, building-by-building pipes, real-time tactics or full courtroom simulation.

LLMs do not determine engine truth. Each complexity must pass a counterexample: does removing teachers make research free; removing logistics let one city do everything; removing performance conditions make free riding dominant? If disabling a mechanism changes no meaningful choice, simplify or remove it.

## Visible decisions and feedback

People and agents jointly plan resources, facilities, education, military and contracts and submit bounded standing policies. Each action shows commitments, actual use, progress, known gaps, deadlines and results—not simply “culture unsuitable.” Unknown causes remain unknown; paid investigation gathers evidence.

[Feedback and RL](../technical/05-feedback-and-rl.md) defines common units, coverage, times, confidence types, briefings and exportable monthly tables. Fast feedback primarily means responsive execution and clear process data. Formal reward remains long-term completion; optional training assistance must prevent farming, hidden-objective leaks and truncation bias.

## Candidate obvious strategies

Permanent expansion of one department without technological/economic complements should lose its advantage through real inputs, absorption limits and displaced future capacity. There is no balanced-budget bonus. A ceiling is not spending; a large fiscal share is not the same as all social resources. See [economic constraints](02-civilization-systems.md).

| Candidate | Required comparisons | Permitted conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All resources to research | Robust/fragile food, sufficient/scarce instruments, peace/threat | Can work with surplus and absorption, but research points cannot bypass inputs |
| Always expand population | Agricultural hinterland, crowded cities, aging, teacher shortages | Scale is useful; ages and service burdens determine when |
| Always maximum education | Course/technology ceilings, teachers/equipment, productive jobs and trade | Endless enrollment cannot bypass complements; short concentration can still be rational |
| Tiny army rushing FTL | Peace/occupation, fixed acceptance tasks, borrowed equipment | Peaceful paths are legal; disarmament cannot shrink tests or erase duties |
| Always raid/default | Immediate gains, substitute suppliers, custody success/failure | Default may be rational at actual risk, never free asset duplication |
| Permanent closure/openness | Substitution costs, risk, absorption and foreign competition | No universally good culture; compare net costs and gains |
| One giant city/complete dispersal | Water, transport, externalities, research cooperation and war | Agglomeration and resilience must change value by context |

No mathematical promise of no optimal strategy is made. A policy should not dominate every resource/opponent/era combination at no cost. Freeze versions before held-out tests across seeds, seats and opponents; retain failures and unresolved games rather than tuning worlds around winners.

## Deliverables and later gates

| Stage | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| D0.4 documents | War/contract transitions, population, FTL acceptance, reward, views/actions/isolation and paper counterexamples; the original release stopped here |
| D0.4 content freeze | Recipes, training hours, FTL loads, population parameters and fixed scenarios; unit/input checks |
| P0, started separately | Rust/TS contract migration, ledgers, state machines, determinism, multicore and isolation; do not implement unresolved conflicts |
| P1–P3 | Subsistence/settlements/education, then knowledge/population feedback, then contracts/diplomacy, with conservation and adversarial cases |
| P4 | Full FTL and integration, a complete short campaign, time reward and unresolved outcomes |
| P5–P6 | Equivalent human panels, batch RL, Mac/web, performance, history and held-out model evaluation |

Logical closure is not numerical balance. Population, health, culture speed, training/losses, investment returns, FTL duration and interaction performance still require prototype calibration. This document does not report those conclusions as verified.
