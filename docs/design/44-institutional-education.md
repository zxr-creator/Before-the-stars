# 44. Education through maintained institutions

Date: 2026-11-13. **Next-design change requested by the user; not implemented.**
This supersedes mandatory repeated course/programme orders in chapters 11, 19, 32 and
later addenda. Real learning delays, population conservation and operating costs remain.

## 1. Player loop

**Build → maintain → educate automatically → employ graduates.** Institutions admit
suitable learners, continue cohorts and graduate people without per-batch orders.
Automatic education is simulation behavior, not a steward action charged against a
turn allowance. Players choose location, construction/expansion, persistent funding
and optional broad focus. National defaults with regional overrides avoid repetitive
clicks. Balanced focus and Standard funding are defaults.

| Institution family | Prerequisites | Continuing output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teaching lodge / apprenticeship hall | Oral knowledge, experienced practitioners, shelter | Practical competence and small teacher pipeline; not literacy or modern science |
| School / academy | Writing, records, teachers and materials | Foundational literacy/numeracy and broad education at actual supported capacity |
| Technical school / polytechnic | Appropriate workshops, machinery and teachers | Industrial/professional competence and maintenance capability |
| University | Scholarly institutions, libraries, instruments, advanced faculty | Advanced education and research-capable professionals, with optional broad science/engineering focus |
| Networked / advanced learning institution | Power, communication, validated teaching and modern infrastructure | Wider delivery and adult retraining, not instant expertise |
| AI-assisted / immersive upgrade | Discovered technology, compute, energy, equipment and supervision | Bounded improvements to supported instruction; no free people or abolished minimum learning time |

Names may vary with culture. Religious institutions can teach if actual staff/resources
and access rules support it. Religion neither provides free graduates nor universally
blocks science. Ancient and medieval civilizations cannot purchase modern attainment
by moving a budget slider.

## 2. Simplified human capital

Use four exclusive broad attainment bands in existing population cohorts: **practical,
foundational, technical/professional, advanced**. Keep age and local cultural/religious
composition. Do not multiply every block by courses, professions and school identities.
These are task capabilities, not a ranking of people's worth.

One person cannot fill simultaneous full-time teaching, factory, military, construction
and mission jobs. Advanced workers can perform simpler tasks at the real opportunity
cost; they are not simultaneously doctors, engineers and pilots. Exceptional tasks
use bounded **on-the-job conversion**, reserving suitable broadly trained workers,
instructors and equipment for a minimum duration. The employer/project schedules it
automatically. Ordinary jobs should not each need a separate certificate.

FTL specialist conversion and commissioning belong inside the funded engine project.
They are real costs and stages, but need no separate E024 order or repeated course
click. A quote identifies missing broad skills, institution capacity or possible
imported teams. No teacher prerequisite may create a closed loop with no bootstrap.

## 3. Automatic service and conservation

Monthly effective places are bounded by the minimum of maintained physical places,
instructor-supported places, delivered materials, eligible learners and relevant
lab/compute capacity. Do not impose a compute requirement on an oral teaching lodge.
Each input/time allocation is counted once. Ongoing cohorts consume resources first;
new intake uses remaining capacity. Enrollment reserves real people. Part-time
apprenticeship uses explicit time fractions that never sum beyond one full-time person.

Graduation requires minimum elapsed time and effective learning. Funding cannot skip
childhood or instantly graduate new intake. Completions become usable in the next
allocation step, not retroactively earlier that month. Teachers come from supervised
practice, existing faculty or actual migration/secondment. Early institutions can
bootstrap from experienced practitioners; advanced ones need the preceding education
base. Reserve a small published share of intake for forecast faculty replacement and
funded expansion; this is automatic, not another annual user decision.

Balanced focus responds to smoothed visible vacancies, replacement needs and funded
construction, subject to capacity. It never reads hidden future technology or FTL
ancestry. Limit intake changes and protect existing cohorts against monthly oscillation.
Employer demand does not create workers. Training may outpace jobs, leaving educated
unemployment and a real opportunity cost.

Education costs construction, recurring supplies, faculty labor and student time. A
budget is a funding ceiling/priority for real services, not a source of free goods.
Reconcile credits with treasury and suppliers; introduce no unconserved parallel
education-points currency. All input claims use the common economic ledger.

## 4. Persistent funding

Provisional tiers: **Economy, Standard, Priority**; three choices rather than many sliders.
Tier changes persist, and regional institutions can inherit national settings.

| Tier | Intended result | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economy | Basic service with reduced supported intake/quality | Slower workforce replacement; existing students do not vanish |
| Standard | Designed ordinary capacity when fully staffed/provisioned | Regular upkeep and teacher/student opportunity cost |
| Priority | Better retention, teaching resources/access and bounded extra throughput | Marginal cost rises faster than extra effective output; space, teachers and era still constrain |

Multipliers require calibration. Priority can be correct in an industrial boom,
recovery or urgent advanced project. It is not artificially penalized. Its cost comes
from displaced power, housing, food and industrial investment or unused graduates.
An empty school cannot produce students or phantom spending.

Show authorized budget, actual spending, unspent budget due to constraints, active
places, projected graduations and employment/shortages by band. Cuts reduce new intake
first; severe underfunding slows learning and gradually increases documented dropout
and maintenance losses. Existing adults do not instantly lose their education.

## 5. Culture, population and military

Access, language support, household obligations, trust and religious institutions affect
participation. Show measured attendance and surveyed barriers, not hidden coefficients.
Education changes culture through years of experience; schools do not instantly convert
culture or religion. Low fertility is not a fixed education penalty: housing, work,
care, security, health and cultural expectations continue to interact.

Migration carries the same people and attainment from source to destination. Imported
teams can be valuable but require real contracts, travel, housing and costs. Teachers
age, die and can be mobilized; conscription competes with future graduation. Military
units use broad recruits plus automatic paid training appropriate to their technology
and organization; population alone is not combat power. Cultural/religious service
constraints remain in willingness and organization, not duplicated national bonuses.

Attainment persists. Refresher practice for a dormant specialized task is distinct
from making every unused qualification decay monthly. Destroying a school hurts its
pipeline and assets, not the education of every past graduate.

## 6. Engine records and settlement

| Record | Minimal state |
| --- | --- |
| Institution | Region, family/stage, level, condition, funding tier/override, focus, faculty, deliveries, effective capacity |
| Policy | National tier, regional overrides, broad focus weights, lawful access rules |
| Learning cohort | Institution, source population references, target band, count, elapsed/effective learning, reserved time |
| Conversion queue | Employer/project, source band, reserved workers, task scope, equipment and progress |
| Education ledger | Intake, dropouts, graduations, faculty changes, costs, unmet inputs and completion estimates |

Coalesce equal-progress cohorts by institution, band and intake period; do not average
different progress into early graduates. Use bounded histograms and cached admission
plans, not an object per pupil. Monthly costs/learning remain settled. Independent
regions can calculate on CPU cores with stable deterministic reductions; no GPU/NPU
is required for this aggregate system.

Order: compute available people/resources → reserve ongoing obligations → allocate
funded teaching and intake → advance with delivered inputs → record exits/completions
→ make graduates available next allocation. Faculty, equipment and power share the
same ledger as industry. Priority cannot claim people already fully assigned elsewhere.

## 7. Migration and acceptance

Existing saves keep old rules unless the owner migrates an unranked copy. Convert old
qualifications to exclusive bands only from actual joint population assignments; never
sum overlapping certificates. Where overlap cannot be reconstructed, retain legacy
rules. Preserve students' paid progress/costs; no simultaneous refund and free graduation.
Remove mandatory `start_program` from the new UI/agent loop; retain it only for legacy
rules. Institutions continue for decades without education-only model consultations.

Test primitive bootstrap; school→technical→university over real time; zero upkeep;
teacher loss; power shortage; no eligible pupils; industry outgrowing training;
migration mid-study; institutional destruction; two employers competing for graduates;
automatic mission conversion; reload mid-cohort. Verify conservation and a working
pipeline without a course command.

Compare constant tiers with bottleneck adaptation on matched starts. Measure productive
employment, educated unemployment, displaced investment, research utilization and FTL
time. Do not assert that balanced spending always wins before measuring it. Correct
large investments are allowed to produce large advantages.
