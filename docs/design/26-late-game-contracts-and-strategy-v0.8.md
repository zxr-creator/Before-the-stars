# 0.8: Late-Game Licensing, Diplomatic Incentives, and Strategy Review

This chapter extends [contract states](14-contract-resolution.md), [complete victory](15-victory-and-ftl.md), and [decision value versus exploits](24-decision-value-and-exploit-boundaries-v0.7.md) with AI, cybernetic, and biological rights, verification, joint research, and political integration. Industries are in [25](25-ai-cyber-bio-industries-v0.8.md); the [postindustrial campaign](../reviews/07-postindustrial-campaign-v0.8.md) is historical cooperative conditional accounting. Its numbers remain historical and are not competitive validation. v1.0 removes shared victory; all current matches have at most one winner. These are design and paper counterexamples, not runtime-tested results.

## 1. Shared knowledge versus scarce inputs

Legally replicable processes or models can benefit several civilizations. This is an important source of valuable decisions. The prohibited behavior is recording scarce equipment, time, authority, or delivery multiple times without corresponding capacity. Preventing exploits must not turn knowledge into a mineral consumable by only one civilization.

| Object | Legitimate sharing | Additional costs or verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public theory and lawfully teachable experience | Multiple learners; teachers retain knowledge | Contact, records, translation, understanding, teacher/student time; new domains still need experiments |
| Licensed documents and model parameters | Distribution within read/copy/modify/sublicense rights | Transmission, storage, hardware compatibility, compute, local validation; copying expands no rights |
| Dataset | Multiple projects within valid rights | Provenance, uses, retention; copies are not independent samples |
| Teaching | One teacher instructs a capacity-limited class | Preparation, contact, practical work, student time; one hour cannot serve incompatible simultaneous courses |
| Compute, labs, reactors | Allocate actual time/capacity across customers | Task specifications, device-hours, energy, samples, supervision, maintenance |
| Administrative/command powers | Charter delegation with bounded scope and traceable responsibility | Authority source, targets, duration, appeal, failure takeover; installing a model grants no sovereignty |

Keep knowledge's returns to scale. Open standards can accelerate adoption, expand maintenance networks, and attract orders while reducing license rents or empowering competitors. Closed licenses can recover research investment while encouraging adaptation and substitution. Actual diffusion, supply, demand, and rival choices decide outcomes; neither openness nor closure gets a fixed universal advantage.

## 2. Rights a late-game license must specify

These subdivide existing `tech_license`, `joint_research`, service, and charter content, not a free-text legal interpreter. Each right references actual document/model versions, beneficiaries, uses, sites/deployment scope, dates, sublicensing, acceptance, and surviving rights after termination. “Acquire AI technology” is insufficient.

| Rights layer | Required definition | Expiry or breach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read and learn | Versions of plans, records, model descriptions, public results | Actual learning remains; unreceived or unauthorized information does not appear |
| Copy and store | Parameter/data/textbook copying, quantity/use scope, custody sites | Copies remain factual assets; lawful retention/use/transfer follows prior terms and executable institutions, never magical remote deletion |
| Execute and serve | Model/process, equipment, tasks, months, local deployment versus purchased service | Services may stop as agreed; independently runnable local facilities do not lose all capability because a foreign server stops |
| Train and improve | Training/adaptation/derivative rights, background versus new results | Provenance and skills remain; continued lawful use follows original terms and enforcement |
| Sublicense and disseminate | Authorized recipients/versions, publication, nonparticipant use | Cannot sell unowned rights or resell overlapping exclusive rights |
| Operating support | Updates, spares, maintenance, revalidation, incident response, mentors, withdrawal | Unfulfilled support enters specific cure/breach; accepted historical deliveries do not roll back |
| Political/administrative delegation | Budgets, equipment, data, commands, duration, review, takeover | Existing charter and actual execution govern; political transfer and technical termination are different events |

Only explicit rights are granted. Reading is not sublicensing; buying weights does not reveal all training data. Conversely, parties may agree broad permanent, self-hosted, modifiable rights. Such rights may be costly, but the engine cannot invent unagreed restrictions to preserve supplier power.

Legal rights and physical access are separate. Rights without delivery do not enable learning. Actual information without permission may create capability plus legal/political risk. Enforcement uses evidence institutions possess, never free disclosure of infringement by an omniscient referee. Physical diffusion cannot bypass actor-workspace/API isolation to read unencountered data.

Immutable content references and provenance identify versions. Renaming or fine-tuning does not erase original rights, known failures, or sources. Equally, all subsequent independent discoveries cannot belong forever to the original supplier. Genuine independent work follows evidence and agreed terms; an infinitely expanding derivative label must not eliminate substitution.

## 3. Contractors cannot choose acceptance tasks afterward

An executable work package fixes task, input domain, quantity, quality/error, equipment, and duration, distinguishing service delivery from guaranteed results. Metrics are comprehensible product conditions, not hidden cultural values, future technologies, or globally optimal policies.

1. **Service:** record compatible device-hours, energy, personnel, data access, and samples. Verified input alone does not prove product quality.
2. **Functional validation:** use agreed independent data, real samples, or operating conditions. Training performance is not validation; buyers cannot substitute an unagreed task to refuse payment.
3. **Local adaptation:** verify language, materials, environment, interfaces, and jobs. Foreign qualification has value without automatically qualifying every local use.
4. **Deployment:** verify real loads, maintenance, error handling, and recovery. One demonstration is not continuous service.
5. **Change:** handle versions, distribution shifts, and revalidation as agreed. Failure under new conditions does not necessarily prove original fraud; hidden known limits or fabricated records are separate evidence-based issues.

Compute always carries `task_spec`. One hundred CB of low-precision screening does not equal 100 CB of high-precision physics. Hardware, memory, bandwidth, and parallelism must fit. Simulation can prioritize expensive experiments and substitute only specified evidence tasks that the engineering acceptance explicitly allows for a validated model/domain. Software predictions cannot replace all physical spaceflight, life-support, or FTL trials.

Independence follows failure sources and real processes, not names. Renamed weights, copied data, and repeated reports add no independent evidence. Identical models on independent machines may provide hardware redundancy without independent software error correction. Test the particular independence a contract requires; do not force all software into incompatible systems.

Ordinary verifiers see acquired disclosure, audits, and tests, not private model lineage. Unknown provenance remains unverified, neither guaranteed independent nor secretly disclosed. Concealment may deceive institutions temporarily but cannot remove correlated failures. Liability and actual performance resolve separately.

Validation sets require actual custody, access rules, and leak records. Public examples can support learning, but leaked repeatedly optimized tasks are not untouched holdouts. New holdouts require samples, specialists, cost, and fair notice. The referee cannot select harder tests because a model is near victory. Errors and failures follow real trial lineage; recreating a project does not redraw truth.

## 4. Partial failure preserves realized value and losses

Contracts retain `scheduled → performing → inspection → accepted → settled`, with cure, dispute, and closure branches. New obligation types do not add an all-failed/all-refunded shortcut.

**Staged transfer:** A escrows 12,000 c: permanent limited-use information rights 2,000, mentoring 3,000, local result guarantee 7,000. Mentoring comprises four 30-hour batches at 750 c each. Documents and two mentoring batches pass; the final prototype fails, and remaining teaching ends. With no other established fees/security disposition, the seller receives 3,500 and 8,500 returns: `12,000 = 2,000 + 2×750 + 8,500`. If the seller spent 2,500 on failed trials, that real cost is not magically refunded from custody; allocation was agreed beforehand. The buyer retains learning/training records without gaining prototype or production qualification.

**Diligent joint-research failure:** A/B/C contribute 6,000/4,000/2,000 c to one account, sharing stage cost 3:2:1. Experiments consume 9,000 without target performance. Contributions bear 4,500/3,000/1,500; remaining 3,000 returns as 1,500/1,000/500. Failure data follows agreed rights. Diverted or destroyed custody instead creates loss/claims; this normal-custody example cannot replenish missing money.

**Contribution failure:** If C misses the next payment, work scales down or pauses under original shares/minimum batches. A's funds are not secretly used to cover it. Presigned capped advances may continue with real debt/rights. Replacements receive explicit future-stage rights, not repeatedly sold exclusive results. C retains vested earlier rights and actual learning.

**Support ends after model delivery:** B may stop uncommitted inference service after expiry. A's paid permanent local-execution model still runs on actual hardware. If A bought only remote responses and never received parameters, it cannot conjure a local copy. These negotiated differences can create large benefits or dependencies.

## 5. Global integration must confront real incentives

### Current competitive rule and historical distinction

The original v0.8 analysis distinguished cooperative shared victory from competitive victory. v1.0 removes the former entirely. Setup fixes four original scoring civilizations, one to four human/external-agent controllers, and native AI filling the remainder. Companies, AI copies, local governments, and renamed countries add no scoring seats or RL trajectories. Actual alliances and charter membership can change, but no agreement creates shared winners or converts the match mode.

The archived cooperative campaign shows conditional engineering and coordination only. It does not establish that competitors voluntarily surrender victory eligibility. Even cooperation requires actual common authority and retained rights; a team label cannot supply global command.

Paying a competitor 10,000 c for permanent ultimate sovereignty is not automatically an attractive offer. If surrender guarantees `-1` while independence retains a nonzero chance of winning, cash or prosperity does not directly compensate the primary score. Do not secretly add submission rewards or reveal another player's private win probability to fix negotiation.

Competition still supports trade and cooperation. A rival may sell nonexclusive information to fund its own critical inputs, share expensive test equipment and later manufacture separately, or accept a charter after military defeat, domestic authority changes, or different beliefs about remaining options. Motives, powers, and evidence must fit the actual situation, not a universal script donating victory. A genuinely defeated seat loses sovereign commands; it does not retain a free perpetual veto. See [competitive authority transitions](33-competition-and-native-ai-v1.0.md).

### What cooperation does not yet establish

| Existing relationship | Not automatically acquired | Required actual arrangement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joint research/free trade | Global political integration | Effective administration, treasury, security, adjudication, retained rights |
| Joint procurement/standards | Ultimate command of members' forces/factories | Mission scope, scheduling, exit, maintenance, staffing, executable delegation |
| Temporary defense alliance | 96 continuous months of global governance | Resident services, disputes, actual authority coverage |
| Military control of the map | Organizational cooperation, skills, FTL | Occupation duties, resettlement, repairs, training, continuing engineering |
| AI administration | Algorithmic sovereignty or automatic public acceptance | Budget, command, appeals, takeover, responsibility; capability and consent remain separate |

Voting shares/membership follow actual charters. Ten proxies controlled by one actor do not automatically create ten votes. If law permits new representatives, fulfill real population/region, authorization, and amendment requirements; anti-shell rules cannot delete legitimate members.

G96 follows actual ultimate control and sustained governance. Renaming government preserves responsibility; changing administrative models does not itself change sovereignty. A real transfer restarts the new holder's G96 under chapter 15. Borrowing power at month end and backdating its return cannot inherit continuity. F24, trials, and governance are separate tests; buying a license does not fill their windows.

## 6. Valuable strategies and responses

| Candidate strong strategy | Potential major benefit | Why it is not universally optimal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Train scarce specialties before factories finish | Avoid 24/36/48-month skill delays | Bad forecasts leave people/equipment idle; materials, energy, or rights may dominate |
| Buy broad rights plus local adaptation | Avoid duplicate R&D and dependency | Seller can refuse/charge more; tacit skills, adaptation, maintenance still cost |
| Validated AI screening before expensive tests | Prioritize scarce instruments | Out-of-domain error, correlated bias, weak validation can concentrate mistakes |
| Common standards/interfaces/procurement | Expand compatible equipment, spares, training | Slow negotiation, unequal contributions, rents, and shared failures invite alternatives |
| Biomanufacturing and high-recovery life support | Reduce some remote supplies or improve health supply | Reactors, nutrients, power, tests, and observation may exceed direct supply cost |
| Negotiate actual global governance early | Overlap G96 with industry and training | Rivals may refuse ultimate control; talks can reveal plans and provoke balancing |
| Two key production lines and replacements | Reduce interruption and single-point resets | Redundancy costs personnel/materials/maintenance; reliable outsourcing can be cheaper |

Benefits arise through actual state and completion time, without extra correct-policy bonuses. Large legitimate gains are not punished. Conditional advantages can be clear; test common starts, shocks, and available information for cross-scenario dominance rather than declaring “no optimum” after comparing three scripts.

AI rule, highly closed ecology, or a cybernetic regime is not mandatory for FTL. Reliable computing, manufacture, energy, maintenance, and governance are required, but organizational forms and industry mixes can differ. Culture/religion names do not predetermine technical superiority.

## 7. Late-game counterexamples and checkable outcomes

Accounting uses `1 c = 1,000 milli_credit`; whole c values simplify paper checks. CB for incompatible tasks are not interchangeable, and every payment needs a real payer/recipient.

| ID | Legitimate benefit or exploit attempt | Rule and example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L01: Nonexclusive diffusion | A and B each avoid 12 months of duplicate research | Both benefit while seller retains knowledge. At 900 c each, seller gains 1,800 and buyers lose 1,800; no extra equipment is created |
| L02: Double-promised compute | A 400-CB machine has 300 reserved and promises another 150 | Only 100 remains; missing 50 is nondelivery. Future expansion is not delivered compute |
| L03: Fake independent validation | Copy 1,000 samples ten times, claim 10,000 independent examples | Still 1,000 source processes. Training-exposed data is not untouched holdout; storage/processing costs do not buy independence |
| L04: Renamed validators | Same weights named Expert A/B both approve one experiment | Two paid inferences are possible, independent model evidence is not. They cannot replace two qualified human practical checks |
| L05: Sell encumbered hardware as free capacity | HW already reserves 300 monthly hours; buyer demands 400 | Ownership transfer preserves use commitments; total remains 400, leaving 100. Legal cancellation follows refunds/breach/release, not hardware duplication |
| L06: Refund retained learning | Documents 2,000 and teaching 1,500 passed, result failed | Accepted 3,500 remains paid; normal custody returns only 8,500, not all 12,000. Continuing lawful use follows rights, not memory deletion |
| L07: Refund consumed joint funding | Account received 12,000 and spent 9,000 | Only 3,000 returns as 1,500/1,000/500. Reports/learning need not be destroyed, and failure creates no money |
| L08: Duplicate water-cycle output | R033 uses 10 m³ wastewater for 9.5 while retaining original input | Original input is consumed. After real use, reprocessing 9.5 yields at most 9.025 plus new power/filters/labor. No nutrients, food, or immortal population appear |
| L09: Copies create labor/votes | Ten AI copies claim ten researchers, votes, winning seats | Copies are information assets requiring compute; population, qualifications, charter representation, and scoring seats do not increase. Real delegation still needs real procedure |
| L10: Rename one line as two | One line capable of one FTL component is registered twice | Machines/hours/materials still deliver one, failing independent-line capacity. Sharing plans is allowed; duplicating scarce work is not |
| L11: Rename or borrow sovereignty | Transfer ultimate control after 95 months and claim 96 next month | New holder restarts G96; pure renaming does not. Trials/manufacture/assets remain, but invalid continuity cannot transfer |
| L12: Ask a model for hidden future tree | Disguise future-node cost query as local inference | Models use acquired data and valid inputs, may speculate with provenance, but cannot read referee truth. Repetition grants no new experiments or research eligibility |

L05 does not prohibit real sale-and-leaseback finance. The seller can receive cash and pay rent to continue use; the buyer pays and receives an encumbered asset. There is still one machine, changed ownership, and actual future cash/maintenance duties. Banning legitimate financing would remove valuable decisions.

L08 checks that recovery variant's input/output only. A second pass needs new wastewater conditions and separate month/facility capacity. Current output cannot flow backward into current upstream inputs. Any same-month closed service must be a content-validated net composite process, not player-created infinite recursion.

## 8. How humans and agents evaluate agreements

Panels/APIs show received rights, versions, document scope, real contributions, outstanding quantities, acceptance evidence, deadlines, used/refundable custody, recurring costs, known provenance, and measurement error. Signing a proposal does not reveal unknown adaptation, private training data, reservation prices, or true political intent.

Players may request sample tests, staged disclosure, mentoring, local installation, third-party review, limited backup, wider rights, and explicit exit. These consume others' capacity and can be refused. Investigation may validate value or reveal dependency. Plans can change, but players cannot freely test every hidden world option.

Technical and political feedback remain separate: “300 of 400 CB delivered,” “two mentoring batches passed, prototype failed,” or “three interfaces compatible, one member has not delegated scheduling” is more useful than one cooperation score. Never show true lie probabilities or hidden culture points needed for assent.

Legal copying, reciprocal licensing, timely recruitment, shared expensive facilities, and costly institutional commitments can transform long-range critical paths. Block fictitious services, duplicated assets, false independent evidence, and scoring identities—not these real gains. Full win rates, dominant strategies, and runtime exploits require implementation and adversarial matches under equal information rules.
