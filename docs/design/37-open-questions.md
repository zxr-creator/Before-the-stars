# 37. Eras, the Solar System, buildings and military: decisions to confirm

Companion to `37-eras-space-buildings-military.md`. Only real choices are listed, each
with the design's decision, the alternative and a recommendation. Nothing here blocks
doc 36 slices 3-6; items 1-5 should be settled before slice 7, items 6-12 before slice 14.

1. **Era ladder and order.** Designed: ten eras, Ancient → Classical → Medieval → Early
   Modern → Industrial → Modern → Information → Space → AI → Interstellar, the last one
   sealed until the objective is revealed. Space comes before AI because orbit (T142,
   tier 7) arrives before validated autonomy (T122, tier 8). Alternatives: AI before
   Space; or eleven eras with a separate "Solar" era for settled planets.
   **Recommendation: as designed.**

2. **How an era is entered.** Designed: understood technologies *plus* a built proof
   (an iron-using town, ten powered works, a running computing centre, a launch complex
   and a first off-world base ...), so the player chooses when to cross and can prepare
   for the transition. Alternative: automatic on knowledge alone (simpler, no timing
   decision). **Recommendation: knowledge plus built proof.**

3. **Traditions ("punk" paths).** Designed: two slots (machine at the Industrial era:
   Coal and Steam, Oil and Engine, Current and Wire; frontier at the Information era:
   Atom, Network, Living Systems, Synthetic Minds), one path per slot, switching by a
   ten-year reform with a 50-year cooldown. Knowledge is never locked; only exclusive
   works and unit variants are. This revises chapter 31 §5 ("not exclusive") for
   buildings only. Alternatives: one slot only; or flavour without exclusives.
   **Recommendation: two slots as designed** (the frontier slot is eighth on the cut list).

4. **Military power per generation.** Designed: keep ×2.5 per generation over eight
   generations (G7 is 610 × G0; powder against mechanized becomes 6.25:1 because the new
   rifle-and-rail generation sits between them), the role counters lapse at a gap of two
   generations. Alternative: flatten to ×2 per generation (G7 = 128 × G0).
   **Recommendation: keep ×2.5.**

5. **Recruiting obsolete units.** Designed: recruiting below the second-best generation is
   refused unless the seat cannot fit out the better one (no goods or no arms-works
   capacity); old kit can still arm militia and reserves. Alternative: allow it with
   penalties only. **Recommendation: refuse** ("no spearmen in the jump age").

6. **Off-world sites and world governance (G96).** Designed: inhabited off-world sites
   count as inhabited regions; colonies pass to whoever takes their metropole's last
   home-world region; autonomy never becomes secession. Alternative: count only the home
   world (space matters less for victory). **Recommendation: count them.**

7. **What the public building tree shows.** Designed: every building, stage, upgrade
   edge, cost, crew, method and output is public; technology and practice requirements are
   shown only as "a technology of the <era> era in <field>" unless the seat discovered
   them; goods are named; the jump works and the Interstellar era are sealed until the
   objective is revealed. Alternative: show technology names (the tree would then publish
   most of the hidden technology graph). **Recommendation: masked requirements.**

8. **Free method switches become rebuilt stages.** Designed: shallow → deep → mechanized
   mines, coke → electric arc steel, scriptorium → print shop, steam engine → turbine,
   vacuum tubes → integrated electronics and similar become building stages that must be
   upgraded (60% of a new building's price, with downtime). This changes ruleset 2.0
   slice D behaviour. Alternative: keep them as free methods. **Recommendation: convert.**

9. **Cities, farmland and food.** Designed: a land budget per region; cities and industry
   take farmland; optional housing saves land; a city's residents can be fed from the
   national pool only up to its provisioning capacity (granaries → warehouses → cold
   stores → logistics hubs, times transport). Alternative: land budget only, no
   provisioning limit. **Recommendation: both.**

10. **World maps.** Designed: the default is a generated Earth-like world (wrapped,
    plates, climates, geology; 24/48/72 regions); the real Earth is an option with 48
    regions built only from public-domain data (Natural Earth, NOAA ETOPO, NASA MODIS,
    USGS) and balanced four-start sets; today's flat generator stays for tests only.
    **Recommendation: as designed**, real Earth after the generated world (slice 16).

11. **Off-world logistics.** Designed: a store per zone (Earth orbit, Moon, Mars, Belt,
    Jupiter, Saturn ...), transfers that need lift or local propellant, travel months and
    launch windows. Alternative: one off-world store per seat with a lift toll (simpler,
    loses the "Moon water makes everything cheaper" decision). **Recommendation: zones**
    (the single store is the fallback on the cut list).

12. **Life beyond the Earth.** Designed: a secret draw places simple life at Europa (45%),
    Enceladus (30%), Jezero on Mars (15%) or nowhere (10%); a ten-year research station
    there discovers it (research evidence, a culture shift, a chronicle moment).
    Alternative: no extraterrestrial life in the game. **Recommendation: include.**

13. **Order of the work.** Designed: military modernization is slice 8, after the building
    lifecycle (slice 7), because refits need the arms-works line. Alternative: insert it
    right after slice 5 (resources and goods), with temporary fit-out capacity at barracks
    (a small rework later). **Recommendation: keep slice 8**, unless military upgrades are
    wanted in play first.
