Against slice 4 (docs/calibration/ruleset3-slice-4-eval.txt):
- Goods chains come into use in historical order: timber, horses and salt in the first
  centuries; tin (bronze) median 3740 BC (49 of 64 seats; the design's "bronze by 2500 BC"
  holds for the median seat that has tin within reach; 15 seats never find or reach tin and
  use copper tools); saltpetre and munitions AD 620-710 (design: by 1600); fertilizer, gas,
  aluminium, magnets and batteries 1735-1820; helium 1860 (design: by 2100); nuclear fuel 2145
  (22 seats); lattice once (the one seat that reached the jump tier).
- Knowledge arrives earlier than in slice 4 (bronze 1760 BC instead of 1400; the scientific
  method AD 1515 instead of 1810; steam 1700 instead of 1870): bronze tools and the second
  extraction camp free labour in antiquity, and the knowledge pace of slice 3 responds to
  the activity of more practitioners. The median seat's writing (2100 BC) and iron (650 BC)
  are close to slice 3.
- Population: higher from AD 1000 to 1950 (the earlier industrial nodes), the world near
  0.39 B in 1950; flat to slightly falling after 1950 as before. The fertilizer works were
  scaled to ten times the design's per-level output (same ratios) after a first batch in
  which intensive fields lacked fertilizer (median seat 59 M in 2100 against 94 M in slice 4);
  with them the median seat holds 75 M in 2100.
- Late-population gap: unchanged in kind; examined with the productivity slice (slice 6).
