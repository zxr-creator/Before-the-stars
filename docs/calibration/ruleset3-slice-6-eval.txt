Productivity by itself, measured (same build, 48 regions, the factors forced to one for a
comparison batch): with the factors at one the batch matched slice 5; with them, before the
corrections below, discovery fell centuries behind (printing AD 1650-1760, the scientific
method 1860-1890) and population fell by half from AD 1000 to 1950. Two bookkeeping couplings
caused it, both corrected: (1) evidence counted heads in posts, so freed workers stopped
"practising" their craft - evidence now counts filled posts at the posts the work would take
at a productivity of one; (2) the town share of a region was 1 - 3.2 x food workers / residents,
so fewer farmers made a town of a village and town mortality rose - it now counts food workers
at a productivity of one. Fields with partial inputs (as many levels at the best method as the
stores supply) replace the all-or-nothing rule that held whole late nations at hand
cultivation for want of a month of fertilizer for every field.

Map size: on the 60-region Earth-like world the same rules develop more slowly than on 48
regions (with no map scaling: printing AD 1585, the scientific method 1800, steam 1875 in a
16-match batch), because seats are farther apart (less contact evidence) and expand later; the
expected +25% research capacity did not appear in measurement (median seat 14-15 regions
against 12-13). Knowledge is very sensitive to the evidence factor: 0.80 put the scientific
method in AD 740 and printing in AD 630; 0.95 (evidence_bp -2000) is the value recorded.
The 48-region map with these rules (same build): printing AD 1415, the scientific method 1630,
steam 1755, electricity 1785; median seat 78 M in 1900, 85 M in 2000, 87 M in 2100.

What remains of the late-population gap, and why (measured, not fixed in these slices):
- Fertility is held down by the food-reserve factor of slice 3 (households judge their stores
  against the government's reserve target, quadratically): in eval-3 at AD 2050 the food factor
  of fertility was 0.80 for the hard seat and 0.46 for a medium seat, with full food access,
  literacy 10-40% and every other factor near one. The native AI's food reserve policy (4-8
  months) thus sets fertility; a late civilization in a calm year reads as insecure.
- Late food supply: the share of working age in food rises again after 2000 (11% in 2000, 20%
  in 2100) and rations per food worker fall from 13.5 to 6: the native AI does not keep
  fertilizer, fuel and horses flowing to its fields at the rate its land could use (one level
  of a works a round per wanted good), so fields fall back to hand cultivation.
- Seats differ widely: in a batch at 48 regions a seat grew from 64 M (1880) to 208 M (2010) at
  1.1%/yr while the median seat stayed near 80 M; wars between seats are rare on the 60-region
  map (one in 25 eval worlds moved a border in the conservation harness).
- Prosperity (slice 11) and the cities slice (9a) are still 0 / stubs in F_city and F_dev.
These belong to the population rules (slices 2-3), the consumption slice and the native AI's
late economy (slice 17); this slice did not change the population rules.
