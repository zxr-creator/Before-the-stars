Ruleset 3.0 slice 6r: calibration repair (no new features).
Ruleset bts-rules/3.0.0-s6r, content bts-content/3.0.0-s6r, generator bts-worldgen/2.0.0-s5,
protocol bts-protocol/2.1 (additions only; see docs/implementation-status.md).
Standard calendar, 60-region Earth-like world, --ai hard,medium,medium,easy, eval-0..31
(32 matches; 16-seed batches were found to shift milestones by a century). Release build,
Apple M5 Pro, all cores (about 2 min 45 s - 3 min 30 s wall per batch).
   target/release/bts run --matches 32 --seed eval --ai hard,medium,medium,easy --curve eval.csv
   python3 scripts/calib_report.py eval.csv
"Median seat" is the median over all 128 seats with land; a seat that never reached a
milestone counts as later than any date (scripts/curve_summary.py, used for the earlier
slices, took the median over the seats that reached it only). IQR = interquartile range
over seats. The world and every seed's world differ between the two batches because the
world label contains the ruleset version.

What changed
1. Fertility's food factor is the households' own food security, not the state's reserve
   policy: access = the region's food history over the past year; security = access x
   (0.7 + 0.3 x max(store term, supply term)), where the store term is months in store over
   a fixed household buffer of two months (at most what the stores can hold, at least a
   tenth of a month) and the supply term is full when this month's fields and works raised
   105% of what everyone eats. The quadratic ratio to the AI's 4-8 month reserve target is
   gone (sim::population, HOUSEHOLD_BUFFER_MONTHS, FOOD_SECURE_BASE).
2. The pre-industrial ceiling: the land factor of fertility is neutral at 46.5% of the
   land's carrying capacity (was 80%), changes 15% per neutral span (was 6.25% per 0.8) and
   is bounded 0.60-1.05 (was 0.80-1.0625); it fades out as children survive (fully gone
   5,000 points of child survival gain above 2,500, i.e. with germ theory and clean water),
   because a modern family is not planned by the land. The development factor's literacy
   term is 1,500 (was 1,800) and its floor 6,800 (was 6,200), a modern fertility a little
   above replacement until prosperity joins it (slice 11).
3. The native AI sizes its supply orders: for each wanted good it orders the levels that
   close the gap between its standing demand (every works at its running method, the fields
   at the best method the seat can use, upkeep, never less than its use) plus a tenth (and
   2% a year of the round's length) and what it makes and receives, up to 12 levels an
   order, halving until the order can be supplied, crewed and powered; works that run well
   but cannot grow where they stand are joined by works elsewhere; the fields' horses,
   fertilizer, fuel and chemicals are wanted as soon as their standing demand exceeds supply
   (before the fields fall back); granaries are sized to the reserve the seat aims for.
   Long rounds keep these as standing build targets (as before).
4. content/pacing.json: a new pacing era "bronze_age" (lead threshold 1.6 M, target 3500-2300
   BC) for writing (T025) and alloy casting (T022); because writing is the first channel step,
   the later eras were re-tuned: late antiquity 24 M (was 20 M), medieval 15.4 M (14 M),
   high medieval 11.5 M (10 M); tier 4 and 5 thresholds x1.5 (0.9 M, 0.96 M), tier 6 x1.25
   (1.0 M). Renaissance, early modern and the scientific method unchanged.

The knowledge-population loop is steep: one variant with the medieval thresholds 1.15-1.5 x
higher pushed printing to AD 2170 and left the median seat at 10 M in 2000 (population
falls, so evidence falls, so knowledge stalls); another with the land factor neutral at 50%
put industry 100-190 years early and the 1800 median at 95 M. The values recorded sit
between them; the late figures move by a third between two sets of 32 worlds (see the
2100-2200 rows of the two last batches below), because the late distribution is bimodal:
a quarter of seats (mostly Easy) stay below 10-20 M while the leaders pass 300 M.

Remaining gaps (not fixed here)
- 3000 BC median about 1.9x the target (four regions settled early hold more than the target
  allows; tightening the land factor strangled one-region starts instead).
- 2000-2200 median 0.3-0.5 of the target and the world peak 0.95-1.2 B (target 1.9-3.75 B on
  60 regions): late food stays tight (the food factor of the median seat falls to 0.78 in
  some decades because production only just meets need), seats diverge widely, and
  prosperity (consumption slice 11) and cities (slice 9a) are still 0 / stubs.
- T024 masonry stays some 1,400 years before table 2.7's classical window (unchanged from
  earlier slices; it is a prerequisite of writing's chain); T029 water mills 170 years early.
- T142 orbital transport about 60 years late; T147 and the jump tier rarely reached (slice 17).

Timing (single match, contention-free, measured after slice 6c; see the 6c file).
